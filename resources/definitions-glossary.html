📖 Definitions & Glossary

Comprehensive Reference: 33 Technical Terms Defined with Everyday Analogies

PART 1: NETWORKING & CORE SECURITY FUNDAMENTALS

1. Cybersecurity
The practice of protecting systems, networks, and programs from digital attacks and unauthorized access.
💡 Like locks, guards, and alarm systems protecting a bank.
2. Confidentiality (CIA)
Ensuring information is accessible only to those authorized to have access.
💡 Like sealing a letter inside a tamper-proof envelope.
3. Integrity (CIA)
Ensuring information remains accurate, complete, and uncorrupted by unauthorized modification.
💡 Like a wax seal verifying a contract hasn't been rewritten.
4. Availability (CIA)
Ensuring systems and data are reliably accessible to authorized users when needed.
💡 Like a 24/7 store keeping its front doors unlocked and staffed.
5. Client and Server
Client is the device requesting data; Server is the centralized host providing the service.
💡 A restaurant customer (Client) placing an order with the kitchen (Server).
6. IP Address and Port
IP identifies the device on the network; Port identifies the specific software application listening.
💡 IP is the building street address; Port is the specific apartment number.
7. Network Packet
A formatted unit of data routed between origin and destination over the Internet.
💡 A single postal postcard with a recipient address and small note.
8. Protocol
A formal set of rules governing how devices exchange information across networks.
💡 A shared language (e.g. English) ensuring two diplomats understand each other.
9. ICMP Protocol
Internet Control Message Protocol (Layer 3) used for network error reporting and diagnostic pings.
💡 A walkie-talkie radio check ("Can you hear me? Over.").
10. Echo Request & Echo Reply
ICMP Type 8 message sent to ping a host, and the ICMP Type 0 reply confirming it is alive.
💡 Clapping hands in an empty hall and listening for the acoustic echo.
11. TCP and UDP
TCP provides reliable, ordered connection handshakes; UDP is connectionless, fast, and best-effort.
💡 TCP is certified registered mail; UDP is tossing flyers out of an airplane.
12. HTTP and HTTPS
HyperText Transfer Protocol for web pages; HTTPS encrypts transmission via TLS/SSL.
💡 HTTP is speaking loudly in public; HTTPS is whispering in secret code.

PART 2: ATTACK TYPES & MECHANISMS

13. Flood Attack
An offensive tactic transmitting excessive volume to starve a target's bandwidth or CPU processing.
💡 1,000 pizza delivery drivers arriving at your doorstep simultaneously.
14. DoS vs. DDoS
Denial-of-Service uses one computer; Distributed DoS commands a botnet of thousands of machines.
💡 One protestor blocking a revolving door vs an organized flash mob.
15. SYN Flood
Layer 4 attack initiating thousands of TCP handshakes without completing them, filling connection memory.
💡 Dialing a restaurant phone, asking for a table, and leaving the phone off the hook.
16. UDP Flood
Sending high-speed UDP packets to random ports, forcing target to look up listeners and send ICMP errors.
💡 Mailing packages to nonexistent rooms in a hotel, exhausting the front desk.

PART 3: PERFORMANCE METRICS & TELEMETRY

17. HTTP Flood
Layer 7 flood sending complex web requests (search queries, database downloads) to overload web servers.
💡 Ordering the most complicated custom cocktail at a bar 500 times in a row.
18. PPS (Packets Per Second)
The count of network packets passing through an interface in one second.
💡 How many individual envelopes drop through your mailbox slot per second.
19. Bandwidth
The maximum data transfer capacity of a network communication channel (e.g., 1 Gbps).
💡 The width of a highway lane allowing cars to travel side-by-side.
20. Latency (RTT)
The time delay taken for data to travel from sender to destination and return back.
💡 How long you wait between asking a question and getting an answer.
21. Packet Loss
Percentage of transmitted packets that fail to reach their destination due to full buffers.
💡 Dropping packages out of an overloaded mail truck.
22. CPU & Memory Utilization
Percentage of compute processor cycles and RAM state currently occupied by running tasks.
💡 How much of a chef's attention and counter space is occupied cooking.
23. Traffic Baseline
The typical, expected pattern of network traffic measured during normal day-to-day operations.
💡 Knowing average morning commute traffic so you can spot an accident delay.
24. False Positive
When legitimate, benign traffic is mistakenly flagged as malicious and blocked.
💡 A car alarm screaming when a stray cat jumps on the hood.

PART 4: DEFENSE, MITIGATION & CRYPTOGRAPHY

25. Firewall
Network security device monitoring incoming/outgoing traffic based on security policies.
💡 A security checkpoint at a gated residential community.
26. Rate Limiting
A defensive policy capping how many requests a user or IP can submit within a time interval.
💡 An amusement park ride allowing only 20 riders every 5 minutes.
27. Traffic Filtering
Examining packet headers and dropping any packets matching known malicious signatures or ports.
💡 A bouncer refusing entry to anyone not on the verified VIP guest list.
28. Traffic Scrubbing
Routing incoming network traffic through high-capacity filtering centers that strip attack traffic.
💡 Passing dirty river water through industrial purifiers before delivery to taps.
29. Load Balancing
Distributing network traffic across multiple backend servers to prevent any single server from overloading.
💡 Opening 10 cashier lanes at a supermarket instead of funneling everyone to one.
30. Mitigation
The process of reducing the severity or stopping the impact of an active cyber attack.
💡 Firefighters deploying hoses to contain and extinguish a building fire.
31. Encryption
Encoding plaintext into ciphertext so that only authorized parties with the key can read it.
💡 Writing a secret message in a substitution code only your best friend knows.
32. Hashing & Digital Signature
Hashing produces a fixed-size mathematical fingerprint; Digital signatures verify authenticity.
💡 A tamper-evident hologram seal and royal signature on an official decree.
